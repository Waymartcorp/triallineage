# Case Page Model

This document defines the reusable structure for TrialLineage case pages, extracted from the Daraxonrasib / KRAS / pancreatic cancer case.

## Core principle

Each case starts with a current medical advance and traces backward through the scientific lineage that made it possible. The structure is layered: it opens with an accessible summary and progressively deepens into the full scientific history.

## Page structure

### 1. Hero / case introduction

- Case title (drug + disease)
- One-paragraph summary explaining what the page does
- Navigation links to each layer (summary, lineage map, timeline, deep dive)

### 2. Plain-language summary

Answers: *What is this case about?*

- What the drug/treatment is
- What disease it addresses and why that disease matters
- What biological target or mechanism is involved
- Why the drug did not appear from nowhere — a brief preview of the discovery chain

Sidebar: "At a glance" summary with the drug, disease, target, timeline, and key obstacle.

### 3. Discovery chain overview

Answers: *What had to happen first?*

A concise numbered list of the major steps that made the current advance possible. Typically 4–6 steps. Each step is one sentence of plain language.

### 4. Reverse-lineage map (visual)

A vertical spine showing the chain of scientific fields from the current trial back to the earliest foundational work. Side branches show:

- Branch points (where thinking diverged)
- Failed approaches (that still taught the field something)
- Enabling methods (tools that made later steps possible)

### 5. Discovery timeline

A chronological sequence of milestones, branch points, detours, and convergence events. Each entry includes:

- Type (milestone, branch point, detour, convergence)
- Era / approximate date
- Title
- Paragraph explaining what happened and who was involved

This is the most detailed section and includes named researchers, institutions, and specific scientific contributions.

### 6. Why this case matters

Two or three short panels explaining:

- Why the case is significant beyond the individual drug
- Why earlier layers of science matter (even when they didn't look therapeutic)
- Why failed or indirect paths still contributed

### 7. Deep-dive view

Longer prose sections expanding on each layer of the discovery chain. Organized by scientific theme (e.g., "Cancer had to become molecular," "KRAS had to be understood as a signaling switch"). Each section is 1–2 paragraphs.

### 8. Related concept pages

Links to standalone concept explainers connected to this case. These pages can be read independently or as companions to the case.

### 9. Footer / about this page

A brief statement explaining the page format and its purpose.

---

## Case metadata (for each case)

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Medical advance | The current drug, trial, treatment, or clinical development |
| Disease | The condition being treated |
| Biological target or mechanism | The core scientific focus |
| Scientific lineage | The chain of prior fields and discoveries |
| Long obstacle or failed path | The major difficulty that made progress slow |
| Enabling tools and methods | Technologies that changed what was possible |
| Clinical translation | How lab evidence became a human trial |
| Broader lesson | What this case teaches about how science works |
| Related concepts | Linked explainer pages |

---

## Tone and editorial rules

- Write for a public audience. Technical detail is available but never required.
- Name researchers and institutions where they made specific contributions.
- Include failed paths. They are part of the lineage.
- Distinguish between milestones, branch points, detours, and convergence events.
- Do not use internal terms (Production Room, signals, workflow, pipeline, backend).
- Do not give medical advice or recommend treatments.
- Cite sources. Every factual claim should be traceable to a primary source.

## Example cases and their metadata

### Daraxonrasib / KRAS / pancreatic cancer (live)

- Medical advance: A KRAS-directed drug enters trials for pancreatic cancer
- Lineage: Oncogene discovery, protein signaling, structural biology, medicinal chemistry, translational oncology
- Long obstacle: KRAS was labeled "undruggable" for nearly 40 years
- Enabling tools: X-ray crystallography, covalent drug design
- Broader lesson: How four decades of work on an "undruggable" protein became a clinical candidate

### Gene therapy for inherited retinal disease (in development)

- Medical advance: Gene therapy for inherited blindness
- Lineage: Disease genetics, viral vector development, retinal biology, surgical delivery
- Long obstacle: Safe and effective gene delivery to retinal cells
- Enabling tools: AAV vectors, retinal imaging, genetic diagnosis
- Broader lesson: How genetic diagnosis and delivery tools turned rare-disease biology into treatment

### GLP-1 therapies and metabolic disease (planned)

- Medical advance: GLP-1 medicines for diabetes and obesity
- Lineage: Gut hormone biology, incretin signaling, peptide pharmacology, metabolic physiology
- Long obstacle: Peptide degradation in the body and the need for long-acting formulations
- Enabling tools: Peptide engineering, structure-activity relationships, large-scale trials
- Broader lesson: How basic hormone biology became large-scale metabolic medicine

### Cancer immunotherapy / checkpoint inhibitors (planned)

- Medical advance: Immune checkpoint blockade for cancer
- Lineage: T-cell biology, immune tolerance, tumor immune evasion, monoclonal antibody engineering
- Long obstacle: Decades of failed attempts at immunotherapy before the checkpoint mechanism was understood
- Enabling tools: Monoclonal antibody technology, tumor genomics, biomarker selection
- Broader lesson: How basic immunology changed cancer treatment

### RNA medicine for rare genetic disease (planned)

- Medical advance: Antisense or siRNA therapy for a previously untreatable condition
- Lineage: RNA biology, antisense chemistry, delivery systems, rare-disease genetics
- Long obstacle: Delivering nucleic acids into target cells without degradation
- Enabling tools: Lipid nanoparticles, chemical modification of RNA, genetic diagnosis
- Broader lesson: How understanding gene expression created a new class of medicines

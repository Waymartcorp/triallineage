# TrialLineage Presentation Standard

This document defines the ideal structure, tone, and quality standard for a TrialLineage public lineage page. Every curated lineage should be evaluated against this standard before publication.

## Core principle

TrialLineage does not merely summarize clinical trials. It pulls readers into the scientific history by showing **why the trial exists now** — what disease problem, biological obstacle, discovery, tool, mechanism, or clinical design made the current trial possible.

A lineage page should feel like a narrative chain of scientific reasoning, not a medical encyclopedia entry.

---

## Page structure

### 1. Trial anchor

Every near-term lineage is anchored to a specific clinical trial.

Required fields:
- Trial name (if available)
- NCT ID
- Phase
- Disease
- Intervention
- Modality (mechanism or drug class)
- Sponsor / collaborators
- Route of administration
- Primary source (typically ClinicalTrials.gov)

### 2. The human problem

Explain the disease burden or unmet need in accessible language.

This is not a clinical definition. It should communicate:
- Who is affected and how severely
- What the disease does to people's lives
- Why existing treatments are insufficient
- Why this disease remains a problem despite prior effort

### 3. The biological obstacle

Explain what made this disease hard to understand, reach, measure, or treat.

Examples of biological obstacles:
- The target protein was considered "undruggable"
- The organ was inaccessible to therapeutic molecules
- The disease mechanism was misunderstood for decades
- The patient population was too small for conventional trials
- The causal gene was silenced by a complex regulatory mechanism

This section should create tension — the reader should understand why solving this problem was non-obvious.

### 4. The scientific clue

Explain the discovery or insight that made the disease mechanism more legible.

This is the turning point in the lineage — the moment when a new understanding made intervention conceivable. It might be:
- Identification of a causal gene or pathway
- Discovery of a targetable receptor or transcript
- A preclinical experiment that proved the mechanism could be manipulated
- A conceptual shift (e.g., from inflammation to aberrant wound healing)

### 5. The enabling tools

Explain the technologies, model systems, assays, chemistry, delivery systems, biomarkers, or trial designs that made the intervention testable.

A scientific clue is not enough — it must become a testable drug. This section covers what bridged biology and clinical trial:
- Drug chemistry or molecular design
- Delivery technology (viral vectors, intrathecal injection, oral bioavailability)
- Animal models that predicted human biology
- Biomarkers or endpoints that could measure effect
- Clinical trial designs suited to the disease

### 6. The lineage map

A digestible chain showing how the field moved from disease observation to trial hypothesis.

Format:
```
Disease observation → biological mechanism → enabling technology → therapeutic hypothesis → clinical trial
```

This can be rendered as:
- A step-by-step timeline (with dates and concise descriptions)
- A set of linked discoveries, each making the next possible
- A visual chain with clear causal arrows

The lineage map is the structural backbone of the page. Each step should feel causally linked to the next — not merely chronological but logically dependent.

### 7. The trial hypothesis

Explain what the current trial is testing, carefully and without implying success.

This should be stated as a clear testable hypothesis:
- "If [intervention] blocks [target], then [measurable outcome] should improve"
- Or: "The trial is designed to determine whether [specific mechanism] translates into [clinical benefit]"

Never imply approval, proven efficacy, or guaranteed benefit.

### 8. Known vs. unknown

Clearly separate:
- **Trial-record facts** — what the ClinicalTrials.gov entry states
- **Background science** — what published research supports
- **TrialLineage interpretation** — what we believe the lineage shows (labeled as interpretation)
- **Open questions** — what remains uncertain or contested
- **What cannot yet be concluded** — guard rails against premature claims

### 9. Source map

Separate source types explicitly:
- ClinicalTrials.gov / trial record
- Peer-reviewed published research
- Government / public-health disease background (NIH, NHLBI, etc.)
- Sponsor / institution statements (labeled as such)
- TrialLineage editorial interpretation (labeled as such)

This transparency is core to TrialLineage's credibility.

### 10. Why this lineage matters

A clearly labeled interpretive section. It should explain:
- What TrialLineage thinks is important about this scientific history
- What broader lesson the lineage illustrates about how medicine advances
- Why the reader should care, beyond the specific disease

This is where TrialLineage's editorial voice is strongest — and where labeling as interpretation is most important.

---

## Heading language

Avoid generic headings when possible:
- ~~Background~~
- ~~Mechanism~~
- ~~Summary~~
- ~~Scientific lineage~~

Prefer headings that create narrative pull:
- "The problem scientists had to make visible"
- "The biological clue that changed the question"
- "The tools that made intervention possible"
- "Why this became testable now"
- "What the trial can and cannot tell us"
- "What this lineage reveals about modern medicine"

Headings should create curiosity and imply causation — not merely label categories.

---

## Tone and accuracy

- Serious, public-facing, educational
- No hype, no promotional language
- No medical advice
- No unsupported biomedical claims
- Careful separation of fact, evidence, hypothesis, and interpretation
- Plain language where possible, technical precision where necessary
- Never imply FDA approval or guaranteed benefit
- Never blur the line between what is known and what is hoped

---

## What a lineage page is NOT

- A clinical trial summary
- A drug marketing page
- A patient recruitment tool
- A news article about a "breakthrough"
- A textbook chapter on disease pathology
- A generic medical explainer

A lineage page is a structured scientific history — anchored to a specific trial, traced backward through the chain of discoveries that made it possible, and presented with editorial care for accuracy, narrative clarity, and public trust.

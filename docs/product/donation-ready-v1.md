# Donation-Ready v1

## 1. Purpose

TrialLineage should look like a credible public science/history platform before actively asking for support. This document defines the minimum readiness standard — the threshold below which requesting donations, sponsorship, or philanthropic backing would undermine trust rather than build it.

## 2. Minimum public readiness standard

All of the following must be true before actively soliciting support:

- Homepage is clean, concise, and immediately communicates what TrialLineage is
- Support/donation page is live and functional
- At least 2–3 strong public lineage pages are visible, with a clear roadmap toward 5
- "Why support this" language is present, specific, and credible
- Editorial-seriousness language (standards, independence, accuracy) is visible
- No unfinished internal language, placeholder content, or Production Room terminology appears on the public site

## 3. Why these requirements matter

A person considering support needs to immediately understand:

- **What the platform is.** Not after reading three paragraphs — within seconds.
- **Why it matters.** Why this particular view of science is worth building and maintaining.
- **Why it is trustworthy.** That the people behind it take accuracy, sourcing, and editorial independence seriously.
- **Why support is needed now.** What the money enables that would not happen otherwise.

If any of these are unclear, the ask feels premature. Premature asks damage credibility permanently.

## 4. Public language discipline

The following terms and concepts are internal and must never appear on the public-facing site:

- Production Room
- Sausage maker
- Internal ranking logic
- Draft-state workflow
- Signal intake
- Candidate type
- Topic pull
- Any reference to backend infrastructure, scoring systems, or internal tooling

The public site should feel like a finished, curated platform — not a work-in-progress dashboard.

## 5. Support page requirements

The support/donation page should communicate:

- **Mission.** What TrialLineage does and why it exists — in one or two sentences.
- **Why public support matters.** That this work is independent, not funded by pharma or institutions, and that support keeps it that way.
- **What support enables.** Specific: research time, editorial depth, new lineage pages, infrastructure. Not vague "help us grow" language.
- **Independence and trust.** That editorial decisions are not influenced by supporters, sponsors, or any external interest.
- **Seriousness.** The tone should be confident, direct, and grounded — not desperate, not casual.

## 6. Public case-page threshold

TrialLineage should not ask for support with only one visible flagship page. A single page looks like a proof of concept, not a platform.

A small but real public library (3+ lineages across different domains) communicates:

- The format works and is repeatable
- The editorial standard is consistent
- There is depth and range, not just one example
- The platform is actively growing

This threshold is not about volume — it is about demonstrating that the model works.

## 7. Next implementation implications

This checklist should directly guide:

- Homepage cleanup (remove redundancy, tighten language, ensure immediate clarity)
- Support page build (mission, independence, specifics, tone)
- Public-page sequencing (which lineages to finish and publish next, in what order)
- QA pass (audit all public routes for internal terminology, placeholder content, or unfinished sections)

When all conditions in section 2 are met, TrialLineage is donation-ready.

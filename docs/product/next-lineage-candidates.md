# Next Lineage Candidates

## 1. Purpose

TrialLineage now needs more visible public lineage pages, not just more infrastructure. The internal tooling (Production Room, intake scripts, news trigger pull) exists to support editorial decisions — but the public site is what builds credibility, attracts support, and proves the format works. The next priority is publishing additional lineage pages.

## 2. Current state

- One flagship lineage page is live: daraxonrasib / KRAS / pancreatic cancer
- Three additional pages exist in draft: gene therapy for inherited retinal disease (milestone-anchored), RNA-targeted therapy for Angelman syndrome, and targeting fibrosis signaling in IPF
- The platform needs to move toward 3 fully visible lineage pages quickly, then toward 5
- Each page should represent a different domain or therapeutic approach to demonstrate range

## 3. Selection criteria

A good next lineage page should have:

- **Strong human-disease anchor.** A specific trial (Phase 2/3) or regulatory milestone (FDA approval, breakthrough designation) that is real, datable, and publicly verifiable.
- **Rich reverse-history potential.** The science behind it should trace back through multiple fields, decades, and non-obvious connections — not a single straight line from lab to clinic.
- **Good public explainability.** The disease, the treatment idea, and the scientific chain should be communicable to a serious general audience without requiring specialist training.
- **Not redundant with daraxonrasib.** Should not be another targeted oncology / kinase inhibitor story. Different therapeutic modality, different disease area, or different scientific lineage structure.
- **At least one non-oncology case.** The platform must not look like a cancer-only project.

Secondary considerations:

- Public interest / awareness of the disease
- Availability of clean public trial records
- Whether the lineage naturally highlights failures, detours, or enabling technologies (these make pages more engaging)

### Decision criteria for promotion from draft to published

- Content is editorially complete (all sections filled, no placeholder text)
- Known vs. unknown section is honest and specific
- No internal terminology present
- Sources section references a verifiable public record
- Page has been reviewed for accuracy, tone, and redundancy

## 6. Why this matters

Getting to 3 visible public lineage pages is more important right now than:

- Adding more intake sources
- Building more internal tooling
- Expanding the Production Room
- Creating more concept pages
- Optimizing search or ranking

The reason: credibility and support readiness depend on visible public output. A platform with one page is a demo. A platform with three strong, diverse pages — covering different diseases, different therapeutic modalities, and different scientific lineages — is a project worth supporting.

Internal infrastructure exists to serve editorial output. If it is not producing visible pages, it is premature optimization.
